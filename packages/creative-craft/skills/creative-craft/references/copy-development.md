# Copy development

Use this reference to write or improve actual copy, including several related
units. Quick Craft can deliver those units directly, without a Copy Sheet,
`cc://` references, or approval ceremony. Facts, exact mandatory language, and
explicit user constraints still govern the work.

For formal Copy Sheet creation, review, Job binding, or public approval in
Traceable Project, read [copy-sheet-contract.md](copy-sheet-contract.md).
That contract remains normative for the formal workflow.

## Find the line's job

Choose what this piece needs to do: make a benefit understood, name a familiar
frustration, invite an action, create an atmosphere, or provide supporting proof.
A headline and body paragraph need not both retell the entire proposition.
Start with the product facts and a concrete audience situation; distinguish a
creative interpretation from a researched audience truth.

Try reading the copy with a competing product name. If it works unchanged,
consider a more specific situation or product relationship. This is a diagnostic,
not a requirement to claim a unique benefit that the evidence cannot support.

### Give the feature a reason to matter

When the job is to motivate choosing or using a product, identify the concrete
want or inconvenience, then explain how a supported feature addresses it.
Naming an audience or occasion is not enough: “下班去运动” does not explain why
someone wants a separate shoe compartment. “鞋有单独一仓” explains the structure
but still leaves its value for the reader to infer.

Follow the reason beyond storage or operation when that is not what the audience
wants to achieve. For a backpack with a separate shoe compartment, separating
shoes from other belongings explains a practical use; changing into sports shoes
to exercise after work can supply the desired destination. In the worked example,
“下班，换双鞋去出汗。” connects the occasion, a product-related action, and that
desire. The body explains the compartment and fixed divider. This is one selected
creative direction, not evidence that everyone needs to change shoes after work.
Physical separation does not establish odor, water, or hygiene protection.
See [creative-contrasts.md](creative-contrasts.md#8-copy-connect-a-desired-activity-to-a-product-action)
for the rejected alternatives and the limits of the user's acceptance.

Choose the motivation before polishing the wording. Test whether the intended
headline and image communicate both what matters to this person and why the
product helps, without the author's explanation. If not, revisit the proposition
rather than shortening a feature label or hiding it in a pun. A wanted pleasure,
ritual, or form of self-expression can also be a reason to choose; do not force
every brief into a problem-solution headline or manufacture anxiety. Purely
informational copy may appropriately stop at clear facts.

## Make language carry the idea

### Fit the consumer's moment

For an everyday product bought for immediate use, identify the concrete want,
situation, or action the copy should address. Connect supported product facts
to that moment in language someone might actually say. If no situation is
supplied, use a modest labeled assumption or a fact-led line; do not manufacture
consumer research, urgency, or a problem the product cannot solve.

A request for sensory appeal does not automatically call for a miniature poem.
An image should clarify an experience, a reason to choose, or an intended
emotion. Scenery invented from the brand name and interchangeable promises of
lightness or delight can consume attention without doing that job. Replace
them with a specific connection when they add no meaning. Brevity alone is not
the goal, and emotionally led briefs can legitimately need poetic language.

Keep the requested placement: a product paragraph, a spoken ad, and a campaign
headline are different deliverables. A reference from a sports campaign does
not authorize turning an ordinary product description into event marketing.
Learn how its wording connects product, occasion, speaker, and audience response;
do not attribute its reach solely to a short line or copy its endorsement claims.
Do not import another drink's electrolyte or recovery benefits, or infer
a non-sweet taste or absence of sweeteners from a sugar-free claim.

### Verbs and concrete relationships

A verb can show what an abstract adjective merely praises. For a fictional desk
lamp with an adjustable shade, “为专注而生” could become “把光转向手边这页。”
The second line connects a supported action to a use; it does not claim improved
concentration or eye protection. An abstract line may still work when the
surrounding image supplies the missing specificity.

### Selection and omission

Choose the detail doing the most work. Do not stack every feature into the
headline, and do not hide a purchase condition, limitation, or mandatory claim
qualifier to make the line elegant. Remove explanation that the image already
provides; add proof where the line would otherwise ask for unsupported belief.

### Rhythm and register

Read the line aloud. Put the important word where the stress naturally falls;
use a pause or a change of sentence length when it changes meaning. In Chinese,
parallel four-character phrases and repeated “让每一…” openings can sound generic
when they replace a specific thought. They are not forbidden forms. Keep them
when they serve the speaker, channel, and intended effect.

Literal translations such as “解锁你的可能” often need a new local situation rather
than a synonym swap. Write how this audience would name the moment, without
assuming slang, regional speech, or internet memes make every brand more human.

### Contrast, implication, and wordplay

Build contrast from a real tension. Let the reader complete a connection when
the missing step is intelligible; explain it when clarity or accessibility needs
it. A pun should support the proposition on first reading, survive the intended
spoken or written medium, and suit the brand. Discard it if explaining the joke
becomes the main job of the copy. Surprise is optional; relevance is not.

### Sensory precision without invented behavior

Use supplied notes, materials, and observations as the boundary. With verified
“乌龙茶、桂花香气”, “乌龙茶里，一缕桂花香” is an expression of supplied facts;
“先桂花、后奶香，三小时不散” adds an unsupported sequence, note, and duration.
Do not invent chronology from an unordered note list. Atmosphere can be imagined
without claiming a new ingredient, effect, manufacturing method, or performance.

## Coordinate the units

Give each unit a useful role: a hero line can invite attention, body copy can
make the benefit concrete, a caption can supply context, and a CTA can name the
actual next step. These are options, not a required four-part deliverable.
Preserve one proposition and a coherent voice while changing detail to fit the
placement. A different word count is not automatically a different copy route.

Honor the requested output format. When a limit matters, check the actual text
under the requested counting convention; if ambiguous, state the convention
briefly. Count Chinese characters separately from English words, and make clear
whether punctuation, spaces, or Latin letters count. Do not assert voiceover
runtime from character count alone; distinguish estimated timing from a recorded
read. Preserve required exact text, qualifiers, and the chosen render method.

## Edit before explaining

Identify the weakest decision: a vague benefit, borrowed brand language, a joke
without a product connection, repetitive units, or an unsupported implication.
Fix that decision rather than polishing every adjective. Read the resulting
copy without its rationale. Keep the version that communicates better, even if
it is simpler or less surprising.

When choosing among alternatives, separate relative preference from readiness
to use. If all versions are fluent but empty, reject the set and revisit the
communication choice; do not promote the least weak version into approved copy.
Use the user's stated reason to guide revision, without making one task's
preference a universal voice rule.

When language remains generic or choosing between alternatives is difficult,
read [creative-contrasts.md](creative-contrasts.md). Show the requested copy first,
then only the reasoning needed to choose or use it. Do not attach evidence labels
to every sentence in an ordinary creative answer; surface material assumptions
and unknowns where they affect the work.
