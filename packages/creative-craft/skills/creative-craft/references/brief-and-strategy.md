# Brief and strategy

A creative brief is a decision contract, not a decorative summary.

## Keep the hierarchy clean

1. **Business objective** — the commercial or organizational outcome.
2. **Communication objective** — what this piece must change in perception,
   understanding, memory, feeling, or behavior.
3. **Audience** — who, in what context, with what prior beliefs and barriers.
4. **Observation** — a fact or visible behavior.
5. **Tension** — a contradiction, frustration, desire, or unresolved tradeoff.
6. **Insight** — a useful human truth that unlocks a creative response.
7. **Support / proof** — why the proposition can be believed.
8. **Single-minded proposition** — the one idea the audience should take away.
9. **Creative concept** — the memorable mechanism that expresses the
   proposition.
10. **Execution** — the image, video, copy, sound, channel, and format choices.

Do not call a product feature an insight. Do not call a visual style a concept.

## Brief fields

A production-ready brief resolves:

- project and decision owner;
- business objective;
- communication objective;
- primary audience and usage context;
- current belief or behavior;
- tension;
- insight with evidence state;
- product/service truth and proof;
- single-minded proposition;
- desired feeling, thought, and action;
- channel and placement;
- deliverables and variants;
- mandatory product, copy, logo, legal, or accessibility elements;
- forbidden claims, imagery, tones, or associations;
- reference assets and their roles;
- success evidence;
- budget, schedule, and production constraints;
- approval state.

## Creative read

Before developing concepts, write:

```text
Reading this as: <communication task> for <audience in context>,
using <tension or insight>, so they <feel>, understand <thought>,
and take <action>.
```

A good creative read is specific enough to reject attractive but irrelevant
work.

## Insight quality test

An insight should be:

- relevant to the specific audience and context;
- tension-bearing, not merely descriptive;
- connected to the product truth;
- generative enough to create multiple executions;
- supportable by evidence or explicitly marked as a hypothesis;
- not a stereotype disguised as psychology.

Weak:

> People want high-quality products.

Stronger:

> People who invest in premium self-care often want visible control without
> looking as if the routine required effort.

The stronger statement contains a tension and can unlock a creative mechanism.

## Proposition test

The proposition should be:

- single-minded;
- audience-relevant;
- believable;
- differentiated enough for the task;
- expressible without execution jargon.

Bad:

> Cinematic premium visuals with dynamic camera movement.

That is execution.

Better:

> Effortless control that remains visible even when life moves.

## Missing information

Do not fabricate product facts, legal claims, performance data, or audience
research. Use:

- `assumption`;
- `working hypothesis`;
- `requires evidence`;
- `out of scope`.

A small task may use a lightweight brief. A campaign or regulated category
requires stronger evidence and approvals.

## Brief lock

A brief can be:

- `draft`;
- `working`;
- `locked`;
- `superseded`.

Generation may begin from a working brief for exploration. Final production
should not be described as strategically approved unless the brief is locked by
the relevant owner.
