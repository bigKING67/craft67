# Concept development

Concept work is divergence under authority, not random ideation.

## Route anatomy

Every route should define:

- name;
- one-sentence premise;
- audience tension or insight;
- creative mechanism;
- hook;
- narrative or communication architecture;
- proposition expression;
- visual world;
- copy voice;
- sound world;
- hero frame or hero moment;
- repeatable content system;
- provider and production feasibility;
- risks and predictable failure modes;
- why it may win;
- what evidence would falsify it.

## Distinctness test

Two routes are not distinct when one can become the other by changing only:

- art style;
- color palette;
- talent;
- location;
- lens;
- camera movement;
- typography;
- music genre.

Routes are distinct when their **mechanism** changes. Examples of mechanisms:

- demonstration;
- transformation;
- contrast;
- inversion;
- metaphor;
- confession;
- ritual;
- challenge;
- proof under pressure;
- social observation;
- visual impossibility;
- serial character world;
- interactive participation;
- product-as-protagonist;
- documentary evidence;
- temporal compression.

## Divergence protocol

1. Restate the locked proposition.
2. List the obvious executions and temporarily set them aside.
3. Generate mechanisms from different families.
4. Build at least one conservative, one differentiating, and one experimental
   route when breadth is requested.
5. Stress-test each route against channel, production, rights, product truth,
   and repeatability.
6. Remove duplicates before presenting.

Do not generate an arbitrary number of routes. Three strong, separated routes
are usually more useful than ten superficial ones.

## Route selection

Different mechanisms can still produce equally ordinary routes. Before
presenting, test the work itself:

- When the task is to motivate choice or use, identify the concrete want or
  inconvenience and how a supported feature addresses it. An occasion plus a
  feature is not yet a reason to choose. Test that connection in the intended
  headline and image, not only in the rationale; label unresearched motivations
  as hypotheses without inventing performance or forcing a pain point.
- Replace the product with another in its category. If almost nothing changes,
  find the missing connection to this product, audience, or situation. A shared
  benefit can still work through a specific execution; do not invent exclusivity.
- Remove style adjectives. Identify the action, relationship, or point of view
  that still carries the idea.
- Ask what the surprise helps the audience understand or remember. Novelty
  that obscures the proposition is a reason to rework, not a bonus.
- Read only the headline or describe only the hero moment. If the idea needs
  the rationale to rescue it, strengthen the artifact before the explanation.

Use these as editorial decisions, not a mandatory user-facing checklist or a
scoring ritual. A clear demonstration or quiet observation can be the best
answer; humor, metaphor, and provocation are options, not requirements.
When routes remain generic or choosing between executions is difficult, read
[creative-contrasts.md](creative-contrasts.md) for worked comparisons. Borrow
the decision principle, not the wording, aesthetic, or scenario.

Selection may use a weighted matrix, but weights must be explicit. Suggested
dimensions:

- strategic fit;
- audience relevance;
- proposition clarity;
- distinctiveness;
- hook;
- emotional or narrative coherence;
- brand fit;
- execution system;
- provider feasibility;
- channel fit;
- rights risk;
- testability.

A matrix supports judgment; it does not replace it. Preserve uncertainty and
state why a route was selected.

## Selection outcomes

- `SELECT`: primary direction.
- `SELECT_FOR_TEST`: competing direction worth controlled validation.
- `HOLD`: useful for a later channel or phase.
- `MERGE_ELEMENT`: borrow a specific element without collapsing routes.
- `REWORK`: mechanism has potential but execution is weak.
- `REJECT`: does not meet the brief or creates unacceptable risk.

Do not merge multiple routes into a diluted compromise unless the shared
mechanism remains coherent.

## Concept-to-system test

A strong route can answer:

- What is the hero asset?
- What are three derivative assets?
- How does the idea survive 6 seconds, 15 seconds, 30 seconds, static, and
  vertical formats?
- What remains invariant?
- What can vary?
- What makes the asset recognizable without a logo?
- What can be tested without destroying the concept?

If the route produces only one attractive frame, it may be an execution, not a
campaign concept.
