# Creative direction

Creative direction translates a selected concept into a coherent production
system.

## Direction layers

### Message

- single-minded proposition;
- headline and supporting copy;
- proof hierarchy;
- CTA;
- exact mandatory text;
- words and claims to avoid.

### Visual world

- subject and product role;
- composition and attention path;
- scale, framing, negative space;
- color logic;
- lighting logic;
- material and texture;
- environment;
- typography;
- brand anchors;
- realism or stylization level.

### Narrative

- hook;
- setup;
- escalation or development;
- turn;
- proof or reveal;
- resolution;
- CTA or end state.

Not every asset needs a complete story, but every asset needs a deliberate
attention architecture.

### Performance and blocking

- who moves;
- where they look;
- body orientation;
- relationship to product, prop, or other subjects;
- pace, restraint, and emotional temperature;
- physical contact and hand behavior;
- entry, exit, and final pose.

### Camera and motion

- shot size and angle;
- camera path;
- subject movement;
- transition logic;
- occlusions;
- speed, acceleration, and rhythm;
- what remains stable;
- reduced or static alternative when required.

### Sound

- dialogue;
- voice and delivery;
- ambience;
- effects;
- music role;
- silence;
- synchronization points;
- rights and replacement plan.

## Reference map

Never submit an undifferentiated moodboard. Each reference needs:

- asset ID;
- role;
- elements to borrow;
- elements not to copy;
- priority;
- rights/usage state.

Example:

```text
@Image 1 — product geometry and label only; preserve exactly.
@Image 2 — warm directional light and shadow softness; do not copy set design.
@Video 1 — camera acceleration and reveal timing only.
@Audio 1 — percussion density and energy arc; not for direct reuse.
```

## Invariants and variables

Lock invariants before producing variants.

Possible invariants:

- proposition;
- product geometry and label;
- character identity;
- hero color relationship;
- end frame;
- campaign device;
- key line;
- sound mnemonic.

Possible variables:

- crop;
- duration;
- opening hook;
- proof order;
- talent;
- background;
- CTA;
- language;
- camera path.

A variant should state what changed and why.

## Treatment structure

A concise treatment contains:

1. creative read;
2. selected route and rationale;
3. audience experience;
4. narrative;
5. visual direction;
6. product/subject direction;
7. copy;
8. camera and motion;
9. sound;
10. reference map;
11. invariants and variables;
12. deliverable matrix;
13. production approach;
14. risks;
15. inspection and test plan.

## Direction lock

Use states:

- `exploratory`;
- `working`;
- `locked`;
- `superseded`.

A model output may explore an unlocked direction. Final adaptation should use a
locked direction or clearly state that concept drift is allowed.
